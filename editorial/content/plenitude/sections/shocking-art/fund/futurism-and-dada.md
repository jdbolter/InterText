Futurism and Dada did not share a single negative program. Marinetti's Futurism affirmed speed, machinery, and war while proposing forms adequate to an industrial age. Dada emerged from the catastrophe of the First World War with a more radical negation, treating coherent programs themselves with suspicion. What the movements shared was a diagnosis that inherited artistic forms no longer answered to the world producing them, pursued through provocation and formal rupture rather than discursive argument.
