There is a growing body of theoretical work and practical applications in the use of LLMs to reimagine writing (Rockwell, Farinola, and Bradley 2026; Roncaglia et al. 2026; Crocetti and Lee 2026; Liu et al. 2026; Lo et al. 2024; Baron 2026). Hannes Bajohr, Luciano Floridi and others have proposed the concept of distant writing (Bajohr 2024; Floridi 2025, 2027), which Floridi argues is the counterpart of distant reading (Moretti 2013). In distant writing, a human author delegates some autonomy to an LLM in creating the text. Some current examples produce static text that is read in the traditional linear fashion by the reader (Bajohr 2023; Marchine, aka Stephen Marche, n.d., Allado-McDowell 2020). Other applications generate text dynamically (Stiles 2025). In some systems, readers enter into dialogue with a model that has absorbed one or more traditional, authored texts, e,g, Gemini Notebook, Rebind, Kindle's "Ask this Book,” “The Living Book of AI,” and “Talk to TR” (Johnson and Houghteling 2026; Lovejoy 2025; Masera 2026; Wang et al. 2026).

For all their innovations, none of the above applications include the reader in the evolution of the text, none allow the text to grow with each reading. By enabling the text to evolve in response to its readers, InterText proposes a different balance between traditional authorship and reader participation.
