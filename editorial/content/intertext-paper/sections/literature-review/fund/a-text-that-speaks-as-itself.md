In most conversational reading systems a model answers questions about a book it has been given, and the book itself
stays on the shelf beside the conversation. In InterText the text speaks as the work: it answers in the work's own
voice, in the third person, about the world the work describes, and the reader meets the argument through the
conversation rather than beside it. The reader is never shown the base text as such; what the reader reads is the
conversation.
