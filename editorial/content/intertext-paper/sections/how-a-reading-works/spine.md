Intertext is designed to support multiple texts. For the prototype we have adapted a text previously published in print, The Digital Plenitude (Bolter 2019).

<!-- intertext:passage how-a-reading-works-p02 -->

The scrollable text is located in the left pane, while the right panes present an expandable table of contents and a space for the reader to type questions or comments. The text is presented in assimilable units of about 250 words each. The reader is invited to respond, after which the system responds with another unit tailored specifically to reader’s questions or comments. The reader may choose to respond again or to continue to the next section or to any other section or chapter.

<!-- intertext:passage how-a-reading-works-p03 -->

Unlike most of the current reading systems, Intertext is envisioned for new texts or texts specifically adapted by their author for digital redefinition. Each Intertext begins from a base text, which is divided into sections and summarized to constitute the spine of the interactive version. In responding to a reader, the model draws first on the spine to guide its response. It may also draw on a fund of extra interpretive or factual units as well as a list of verified citations and a set of illustrative images and videos. The model can also search the web for further information.

<!-- intertext:passage how-a-reading-works-p04 -->

Intertext’s responses are generated by prompting Claude Sonnet 5.5. Each prompt is a bundle consisting of summaries of the argument and previous reader responses together with fixed rules for responding. Claude’s initial completion is then revised for style before being delivered to the reader. The two passes are designed:

- To ensure that the responses do not exhibit random drift from the base argument.
- To maintain the tone as close as possible to the author’s text and limit stylistic tics of LLMs.
- To regulate the citation of outside works from a verified library of references.
