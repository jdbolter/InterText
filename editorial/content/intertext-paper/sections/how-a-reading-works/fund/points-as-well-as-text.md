Besides the spine, each section and each larger division of the work carries a short statement of its argument,
written or approved by the author and never shown to the reader. The full text supplies the material; the statement
supplies the direction. A section's text gives a passing example and its thesis roughly equal weight, and a model
answering a question about a detail tends to follow the detail. The statement tells it what is central. It steers
every reply, and it is stated only when the reader's message bears on it, in fresh words, as a claim.
