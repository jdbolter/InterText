Before you begin

What you are about to read has already been shaped, in part, by the readers who came before you. When your conversation with the historian surfaces a plausible consequence, a named figure, or a connection the record hadn't drawn, that exchange can be folded back into the archive itself. The version the next reader finds will have been quietly changed by someone they will never meet.

If you agree, your conversation today will be used to enrich the sections you engage with — anonymously, as one contribution among many to a record that continues to evolve with its readers.

You can of course decline. The reading experience will be the same either way.
