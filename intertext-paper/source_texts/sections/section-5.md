## Synthetic Readers

The system has undergone tuning and alpha testing by using synthetic (AI-powered) readers, which work through the text as human readers would, offering questions and comments. Each reader has been given their own profile. Such characterization of AI agents is a growing practice (Park et al. 2023; Argyle et al. 2023; Hämäläinen, Tavast, and Kunnari 2023; Masera 2026).

The Intertext system of characterization was derived from Freeman (2026). There are currently five profiles: general, collaborative, curious, skeptical, and student. The readers are powered by a different model (OpenAI GPT 5.6 terra), while the texts are generated by Claude Sonnet as noted above. The resultant readings (text and responses) may then be examined by a separate Claude agent and by the human author to evaluate the flow of the argument, the use of fund materials, links and graphics, and the quality of the prose.

These synthetic readers have shown that the model can absorb the argument of the text and apply it to new issues or examples raised by readers’ questions. The reader system has also helped the author to rethink the original argument and its evidence. For example, in The Digital Plenitude, some readers objected to the text’s formulation that traditional hierarchies in arts and culture have “almost, but not quite, vanished.” The system then drafted a more nuanced version. If this objection had occurred during a run with human readers, the spine or fund could have been changed for subsequent readers. The synthetic readings also suggested stylistic improvements to reduce the awkward wordings such as “the text claims.”
