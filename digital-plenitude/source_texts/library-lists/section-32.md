[ulrich-2000] Ulrich, "Nobody Else Works for Free" (2000). From the work's own use: as a carpenter decides whether to keep, sell or give away a table, "My band authored the music which is Napster's lifeblood. We should decide what happens to it, not Napster." Use: the popular modernist case for the artist as sole creator and owner.

[right2remix-2017] Right2Remix.org, "Manifesto" (2017). From the work's own use (cite only for these points): "We live in an age of remix," "everything is a remix," and since creative copying has become commonplace, the right to remix is a fundamental requirement for freedom of expression. Use: the denial of originality as an aesthetic and legal foundation.

[jameson-1991] Jameson, Postmodernism (1991). With the end of personal style, pastiche takes the place of parody (pp. 16–17). Use: pastiche, appropriating earlier styles, as the art world's mark of postmodernism, and the borrowing that remix shares with it.

[lessig-2004] Lessig, Free Culture (2004). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): the music and film industries have pursued a definition of copyright under which sampling even a few seconds of another work is infringement; the book was released free under a Creative Commons license. Use: the legal case for a right to remix.

[mcleod-2015] McLeod, "An Oral History of Sampling" (2015). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): in the early 1990s lawsuits, among them one against Biz Markie for looping "Alone Again (Naturally)," led the music industry to expect every sample to be licensed (p. 83). Use: how commercial sampling became costly, while amateurs ignored the rules.

[sonvilla-weiss-2015] Sonvilla-Weiss, "Good Artists Copy; Great Artists Steal" (2015). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): the saying that great artists steal has been credited to Picasso and Stravinsky as well as to Eliot (p. 57). Use: modernists who borrowed and still held fast to originality.

[eliot-1921] Eliot, "Philip Massinger" (1921). From the work's own use: "Immature poets imitate; mature poets steal; bad poets deface what they take, and good poets make it into something better, or at least something different. The good poet welds his theft into a whole of feeling which is unique." Use: a modernist's theft, which must still yield a unique work, against remix's sampling of recent hits.

[krauss-1981] Krauss, "The Originality of the Avant-Garde" (1981). The avant-garde's cult of originality rests on a repressed logic of the copy, which postmodern appropriation exposes (pp. 65–66). Use: originality already in question within the art world, before remix questioned it in popular culture.

[boon-2010] Boon, In Praise of Copying (2010). From a description of the book (cite only for these points): copying is fundamental to culture and learning, and the line between copy and original is a practical convenience rather than a basic truth. Use: a later philosophical ally of remix; the work does not cite him.

[gunkel-2016] Gunkel, Of Remixology (2016). From the publisher's description (cite only for these points): advocates and critics of remix share the values of originality and integrity, and understanding remix needs a new theory of value rather than a defence of originality. Use: why originality and remix are a spectrum, not a simple opposition; the work does not cite him.
