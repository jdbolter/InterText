[smith-brenner-2012] Smith and Brenner, "Twitter Use 2012" (2012). As corrected against the survey (cite only for these points): 15 percent of online adults used Twitter, 26 percent of those 18 to 29, and 31 percent of those 18 to 24. Use: flow culture as, in part, youth culture.

[carrier-2018] Carrier, Rosen and Rokkum, "Productivity in Peril" (2018). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): those born after 1980 multitask more than Generation X, which does so much more than the baby boomers. Use: generations and multitasking.

[bull-2013] Bull, "iPod Use: An Urban Aesthetics of Sonic Ubiquity" (2013). From the work's own use: young urban users "appear to intuitively tune their flow of desire and mood to the spectrum of music contained in their iPods" (p. 501). Use: personal music as a flow experience.

[manovich-2001] Manovich, The Language of New Media (2001). From the work's own use: "After the novel, and subsequently cinema, privileged narrative as the key form of cultural expression of the modern age, the computer age introduces its correlate—the database. Many new media objects do not tell stories; they do not have a beginning or end" (p. 218). Use: YouTube as database cinema.

[manovich-2002] Manovich, Soft Cinema (2002). From the opening pages: in "database cinema," a large database and editing rules can generate a potentially unlimited number of films (p. 3). Use: Manovich's own attempt at database cinema, beside YouTube's.

[burgess-2018] Burgess and Green, YouTube (2018). From the publisher's description (cite only for these points): YouTube's amateur culture and its commercial logics have coexisted in tension, producing new genres and professions. Use: YouTube's flow as both participatory and commercial.

[csikszentmihalyi-1991] Csikszentmihalyi, Flow (1991). From a summary and the work's own use: in a secular and often hostile world, flow gives individuals a feeling of control in smaller domains, "the process of achieving happiness through control over one's inner life" (p. 6). Use: flow as therapeutic.

[boyd-2014] boyd, It's Complicated (2014). From the publisher's description and the work's own use (cite only for these points): teens integrate social media into their social lives and present different identities to different audiences. Use: social media as identity construction, which chapter 8 takes up.

[chun-2016] Chun, Updating to Remain the Same (2016). From the publisher's description (cite only for these points): new media matter most when they become habitual, and people act like their machines, streaming, updating and sharing, "updating to remain the same." Use: the homeostatic identity of flow culture.
