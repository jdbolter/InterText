[johnson-2006] Johnson, Everything Bad Is Good for You (2006). From the work's own use: "It's not what you're thinking about when you're playing a game, it's the way you're thinking that matters. … Games force you to decide, to choose, to prioritize." Use: games as training in procedural thinking.

[mcgonigal-2011] McGonigal, Reality Is Broken (2011). From the work's own use: collaborative games could "change the world": "Very big games represent the future of collaboration," and they are the best hope for solving the most complex problems of our time. Use: games as salvation, as art was for the avant-garde.

[levy-1997] Lévy, Collective Intelligence (1997). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): Lévy first advanced the notion of collective intelligence. Use: the idea behind McGonigal's collaborative games.

[rose-2011] Rose, The Art of Immersion (2011). From the work's own use: the alternate reality game for The Dark Knight made its players accomplices to the Joker's crime; the first ARGs threw out a story and let an online audience reconstruct it (p. 11). Use: Nolan's Joker as an artist of the alternate reality game.

[bogost-2007] Bogost, Persuasive Games (2007). Procedurality is the computer's principal value, creating meaning through the interaction of algorithms (p. 4). Use: parameters and the event loop as procedures the player inhabits.

[sicart-2014] Sicart, Play Matters (2014). From the publisher's description (cite only for these points): play is a mode of being in the world, not an activity bound to games. Use: a later case that play reaches far beyond games; the work does not cite him.
