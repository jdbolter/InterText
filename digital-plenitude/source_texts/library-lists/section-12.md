[mcluhan-1964] McLuhan, Understanding Media (1964). Each medium, by its technical form rather than its content, extends the senses and changes human nature and culture; "the medium is the message" is the title of the first chapter. An improved, higher-definition television image would no longer be television (p. 273); television, a cool medium, favoured casual, approachable performers (pp. 288–289). Use: McLuhan's own claims, in his own terms.

[mcluhan-1962] McLuhan, The Gutenberg Galaxy (1962). From a summary (cite only for these points): print favoured linear, individualist thought; electronic media restore a collective interdependence on a world scale, the "global village". Use: the procession of media in McLuhan's history.

[greenberg-1960] Greenberg, "Modernist Painting" (1960). From a summary (cite only for these points): each art secures its standards by purging effects borrowed from the other arts, so that its uniqueness lies in its medium. Use: the elite source of the medium specificity that McLuhan carried into popular culture.

[murray-1997] Murray, Hamlet on the Holodeck (1997). From the author's notes: the digital environment has four essential properties, procedural, participatory, spatial and encyclopedic (p. 67). Use: a digital writer seeking the essence of the computer medium, as McLuhan sought television's.

[bogost-2007] Bogost, Persuasive Games (2007). Procedurality is the computer's principal value, creating meaning through the interaction of algorithms (p. 4); games persuade through the processes they model, which he calls procedural rhetoric. Use: the medium-specific argument for the computer.

[danto-1997] Danto, After the End of Art (1997). In the art world, painting became one medium among many, alongside installation, performance, video and craft (p. 136). Use: medium specificity was ending among the elite arts just as McLuhan made it popular.

[peters-2016] Peters, The Marvelous Clouds (2016). Media are infrastructures and forms of life, not only channels of messages (pp. 13–14). Use: a later philosopher of media who keeps McLuhan's claim that media shape life, without the promise of one final medium.

[bolter-2022] Bolter, "The Condition of Reading in a Digital Media Culture" (2022). Later work by the book's own author, carrying this argument on; its ideas are the work's own. Heterogeneity, not convergence, defines contemporary media culture, though the digital inflects how all media are understood (p. 36). Use: against the claim that the computer is the final medium.
