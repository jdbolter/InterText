[danto-1997] Danto, After the End of Art (1997). Art with a master narrative runs from the Renaissance to the end of modernism (pp. 4–5); after it, no narrative imposes limits and anything can be art (p. 47). Use: the end of jazz's and rock's shared trajectories, which the section compares with Danto's end of art.

[bourdieu-1984] Bourdieu, Distinction (1984). From the publisher's description (cite only for this point): judgements of taste are socially structured, and distinctions of taste reproduce distinctions of class. Use: the "cultural capital" that rock developed in Greene's phrase is Bourdieu's term; the canon that crowned rock as art was a social as well as an aesthetic judgement.

[macdonald-1962] Macdonald, "Masscult and Midcult" (1960; page numbers from its 1962 printing in Against the American Grain). Midcult wears High Culture's standards while watering them down (pp. 37–40). Use: an opponent's view, from the side of High Culture, of popular forms that claim the standing of art, such as a rock album.

[greenberg-1939] Greenberg, "Avant-Garde and Kitsch" (1939). From a summary (cite only for these points): kitsch is commercial culture that loots a mature tradition's discoveries, and its profits tempt the avant-garde. Use: how an elite modernist would have judged jazz and rock seeking to be art.

[jameson-1991] Jameson, Postmodernism (1991). The postmodernisms efface the older high-modernist frontier between high culture and mass or commercial culture (pp. 2–3). Use: the frontier that jazz and then rock crossed.

[huyssen-1986] Huyssen, After the Great Divide (1986). From the book's contents (cite only for this point): the book's first part treats mass culture as modernism's other, including the essay "Mass Culture as Woman: Modernism's Other". Use: why popular music had to win recognition from the elite arts.
