[shiner-2001] Shiner, The Invention of Art (2001). The book itself calls him Larry Shiner. The crafts were demoted from the fine arts in the eighteenth century, the fine arts being defined by inspiration and genius, the crafts by skill, rules and use (p. 5). Shiner registers recent crossings between fine art, craft and popular forms and the popularisation of the idea of the artist (pp. 281, 286–288). Use: the reunion of art and craft as the great divide closed.

[danto-1997] Danto, After the End of Art (1997). After the end of modernism's master narrative, anything can be art (p. 47); painting became one medium among many, alongside installation, performance, video and craft (p. 136). Use: the broadening of art to almost every form of creativity.

[lanier-2010] Lanier, You Are Not a Gadget (2010). From the author's notes and the book's own reading: Web 2.0 software degrades the idea of the person, and the remix and social-networking cultures have not produced artists of the kind he admires. Use: an opponent, one of the critics the section names, who say that this leveling erases the difference between good work and poor.

[keen-2007] Keen, The Cult of the Amateur (2007). From the author's notes: the Web 2.0 revolution promised more truth to more people, but delivers superficial analysis and shrill opinion instead of considered judgement, the noise of "a hundred million bloggers all simultaneously talking about themselves" (p. 16). Use: an opponent, named in this section beside Lanier, who holds that the leveling of amateur creativity leaves no way to tell good work from poor.

[lessig-2008] Lessig, Remix (2008). The twentieth century professionalized popular culture into a "Read/Only" culture (pp. 28–29); digital technologies make "Read/Write" culture possible again (pp. 33–35). Use: the layered creative practice of digital media, open to ever more participants.

[bogost-2007] Bogost, Persuasive Games (2007). Procedurality is the computer's principal value, creating meaning through the interaction of algorithms (p. 4). Use: the key quality that popular modernists see in digital technology, the successor of the Futurists' speed.

[park-2024] Park, "The Work of Art in the Age of Generative AI" (2024). From the abstract (cite only for these points): generative AI democratizes artistic expression, as mechanical reproduction did, while creating new dependencies. Use: a development after 2019 that extends the artist and designer "in all of us" to anyone with a prompt.

[bolter-2022] Bolter, "The Condition of Reading in a Digital Media Culture" (2022). Later work by the book's own author, carrying this argument on; its ideas are the work's own. Social media let readers pass constantly into writing; there may now be more "published" authors than readers (p. 39). Use: the multiplying ways to be creative in digital media.
