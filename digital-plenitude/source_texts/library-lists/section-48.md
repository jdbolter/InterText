[pew-2014] Pew Research Center, "Political Polarization in the American Public" (June 12, 2014). From the report: it documents growing ideological consistency and partisan division among the American public. Use: polarization of the electorate; it does not by itself establish that political narratives have become less coherent.

[skocpol-williamson-2012] Skocpol and Williamson, The Tea Party and the Remaking of Republican Conservatism (2012). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): Tea Party Republicans affected to hate the federal government yet saw Social Security and Medicare as justified entitlements. Use: party narratives less consistent than their polarization suggests.

[hochschild-2016] Hochschild, Strangers in Their Own Land (2016). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): the community she studied in Calcasieu Parish, Louisiana, held confused political positions, explained by what she called a "deep story," really a myth of identity. Use: political feeling without a coherent narrative.

[pearce-2016] Pearce, "Trump Has Spent a Fraction of What Clinton Has on Ads" (2016). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): Clinton spent much more heavily on television advertising. Use: the older media of the cathartic campaign.

[bump-2018] Bump, "All the Ways Trump's Campaign Was Aided by Facebook" (2018). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): the Trump campaign's use of Facebook and Twitter was more effective at reaching and motivating targeted voters. Use: social media as the matrix of the politics of flow.
