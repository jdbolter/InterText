[wolfe-1976] Wolfe, "The 'Me' Decade and the Third Great Awakening" (1976). The new dream of the 1970s is changing and polishing one's own self (p. 1). Use: the Me Decade, an earlier complaint of narcissism than the one aimed at social media.

[lasch-1978] Lasch, The Culture of Narcissism (1978). From the publisher's description and the work's own use: narcissism has become a social condition of American life, and "the contemporary climate is therapeutic, not religious." Use: the other 1970s diagnosis; Lasch could see the end of traditional hierarchies only as decline, so say so when citing him.

[brooks-2016] Brooks, "Narcissism Is Increasing. So You're Not So Special" (2016). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): psychologists report that narcissism has been increasing, and Brooks calls ours the age of the New Narcissism. Use: the conservative complaint the section sets against its 1970s precedents.

[firestone-2012] Firestone, "Is Social Media to Blame for the Rise in Narcissism?" (2012). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): a writer in Psychology Today asks whether social media are to blame. Use: the popular form of the complaint.

[csikszentmihalyi-1991] Csikszentmihalyi, Flow (1991). Flow is a state of involvement so complete that nothing else seems to matter (p. 4); in a secular world it gives individuals "control over one's inner life" (p. 6). Use: flow as a secular version of the Me generation's spirituality and a therapy without shared values.
