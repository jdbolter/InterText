[mcgonigal-2017] McGonigal, "Gaming Can Make a Better World" (TED talk). From the work's own use: a gamer about to achieve an "epic win" shows "a classic games emotion … [a] sense of urgency, a little bit of fear, but intense concentration, deep, deep focus on tackling a really difficult problem." Use: the intensity of engagement games generate.

[mcgonigal-2011] McGonigal, Reality Is Broken (2011). From the author's notes (cite only for these points): games fulfil genuine human needs that the real world does not, and can be harnessed to make people happier and more resilient and to solve real-world problems. Use: the belief that games can change the world, set beside the real intensity of play.

[schell-2008] Schell, The Art of Game Design (2008). From the work's own use: "it pays for game designers to make a careful study of flow, because this is exactly the feeling we want the players of our games to enjoy" (p. 118); flow activities must stay in "the narrow margin of challenges that lies between boredom and frustration," which Csikszentmihalyi calls "the flow channel" (p. 119). Use: designers who deliberately cultivate flow.

[chen-2007] Chen, "Flow in Games (and Everything Else)" (2007). From the work's own use: descriptions of flow "are identical to what players experience when immersed in games," and gamers value games by whether they provide it (p. 32). Use: a designer's testimony that flow is what games are for.

[schrank-2014] Schrank, Avant-Garde Videogames (2014). From the abstract and the work's own use: "Despite the range of things to be considered when developing games that flow, the aim is always the same: players lose self-consciousness in the enveloping action" (p. 33). Use: the aim of mainstream games, which the avant-garde games of section 29 refuse.

[csikszentmihalyi-1991] Csikszentmihalyi, Flow (1991). From a summary and the work's own use: flow is a state of involvement so complete that nothing else seems to matter, pursued for its own sake (p. 4). Use: the psychology that game designers draw on.

[esa-2017] Entertainment Software Association, "Sales, Demographic and Usage Data" (2017). From the work's own use, as corrected and checked (cite only for these points): women over eighteen make up 31 percent of all game players, and the average female player is 37. Use: who plays.

[danastasio-2017] D'Anastasio, "Study Shows Which Video Game Genres Women Play Most" (2017). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): women account for about 70 percent of the players of matching games like Tetris. Use: who plays casual games.

[bogost-2007] Bogost, Persuasive Games (2007). From the abstract and the work's own use: games persuade through the processes they model, procedural rhetoric, and serious games are used in education, health and politics (p. 4 for procedurality). Use: games that have become "serious."

[polaine-2005] Polaine, "The Flow Principle in Interactivity" (2005). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): the user becomes part of the event loop that drives a program's action, so that she not only experiences flow but becomes part of the program's flow. Use: why digital media lend themselves to flow.
