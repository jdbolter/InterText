[jameson-1991] Jameson, Postmodernism (1991). The postmodernisms efface the high-modernist frontier between high and mass culture (pp. 2–3). Postmodernism is a cultural dominant, not a style, within which residual and emergent forms coexist (pp. 4–6). With the end of personal style, pastiche takes the place of parody (pp. 16–17), and the nostalgia film conveys "pastness" through style (pp. 19–20). Use: the section's own source on postmodernism.

[krauss-1981] Krauss, "The Originality of the Avant-Garde" (1981). The avant-garde's cult of originality rests on a repressed logic of the copy, which postmodern appropriation, such as Sherrie Levine's rephotographs, exposes (pp. 65–66). Use: postmodernism's play with modernism's fragments, against Jobs's insistence on originality.

[burger-1984] Bürger, Theory of the Avant-Garde (1984). Since the avant-garde, the radically disparate exists side by side, and no movement can claim to be historically more advanced than any other (p. 63). Use: with no mainstream, there can be no avant-garde.

[danto-1997] Danto, After the End of Art (1997). After the end of modernism's master narrative, anything can be art (p. 47). Use: why no new "ism" can name the whole.

[lessig-2008] Lessig, Remix (2008). Digital technologies make a "Read/Write" culture possible again (pp. 33–35); Girl Talk's Night Ripper is built from some 200 to 250 samples (pp. 11–15). Use: the sampling and remix that Jobs had no use for, which chapter 6 takes up.

[bolter-2022] Bolter, "The Condition of Reading in a Digital Media Culture" (2022). Later work by the book's own author, carrying this argument on; its ideas are the work's own. To its users the web appears flat, without centre or periphery, an operational metaphor for a culture without a universally agreed centre or a single canon (pp. 38–39). Use: there is no dominant cultural story today.
