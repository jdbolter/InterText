[heilig-1955] Heilig, "The Cinema of the Future" (1955; reprinted 1992). The new screen formats of the 1950s, Cinerama, CinemaScope and 3-D, are steps toward a cinema that would engage every sense (pp. 279–280). Use: an earlier promise of total cinema, the logic of remediation that VR film repeats.

[rogers-2013] Rogers, Cinematic Appeals (2013). From the book's contents (cite only for this point): each new cinema technology, widescreen and 3-D in the 1950s, digital cinema in the 1990s, 3-D again, was promoted through claims about the spectator's experience. Use: Milk's "empathy machine" as the latest of these claims.

[henrickson-2025] Henrickson, Digital Storytelling (2025). Immersive media curate interactions with space or alter our perception of it; Jaron Lanier has called virtual reality "a consciousness-noticing machine," which makes us more aware of our own embodied experience (p. 91). Use: a counter to the empathy machine: immersion that turns the viewer back on herself rather than into identification.

[murray-1997] Murray, Hamlet on the Holodeck (1997). From the author's notes: immersion, agency and transformation are the digital medium's characteristic pleasures. Use: VR film offers immersion without agency, and so remains film.

[milk-2015b] Milk, "How Virtual Reality Can Create the Ultimate Empathy Machine" (TED talk, 2015). From the work's own use (cite only for these points): VR becomes a machine that "feels like truth"; Milk took Clouds over Sidra to the World Economic Forum in Davos so that political and economic elites could experience a refugee's daily life. Use: VR as the empathy machine.

[milk-2015a] Milk, Clouds over Sidra (2015). From the work's own use (cite only for these points): a 360-degree documentary about a young Syrian refugee in a Lebanese camp, made with the United Nations, whose techniques are mostly those of documentary film. Use: the section's example of VR documentary.

[johnson-2015] Johnson, "Virtual Reality Is 'The Last Medium,' Says Filmmaker and Vrse CEO Chris Milk" (2015). From the work's own use: Milk believes his form of filmmaking can "engage an audience on a deeper human level than any other medium that has reached them." Use: Milk's claim for VR film, in his own words.

[engberg-bolter-2017] Engberg and Bolter, "Mobile Cinematics" (2017). The work's own earlier thinking (cite only for this point): VR film remediates documentary film as an experience more compelling and more emotionally engaging, because it locates the viewer in the scene; a classic argument of remediation. Use: the work's own account of VR film as remediation.
