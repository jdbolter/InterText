[csikszentmihalyi-1991] Csikszentmihalyi, Flow (1991). From a summary and the book's own reading: flow is a state of involvement so complete that nothing else seems to matter, pursued for its own sake (p. 4); it requires concentration and a match between challenge and skill. Use: flow, one pole of the first dichotomy, which chapter 5 takes up.

[lessig-2008] Lessig, Remix (2008). Girl Talk's Night Ripper is built from some 200 to 250 samples (pp. 11–15); digital technologies make "Read/Write" culture possible again (pp. 33–35). Use: remix, one pole of the second dichotomy, which chapter 6 takes up.

[gunkel-2016] Gunkel, Of Remixology (2016). From the publisher's description (cite only for these points): advocates and critics of remix share the values of originality and integrity, and understanding remix needs a new theory of value rather than a defence of originality. Use: why originality and remix are a spectrum, not a simple opposition.

[krauss-1981] Krauss, "The Originality of the Avant-Garde" (1981). The avant-garde's cult of originality rests on a repressed logic of the copy, which postmodern appropriation exposes (pp. 65–66). Use: originality, the elite pole of the second dichotomy, already in question within the art world.

[bogost-2007] Bogost, Persuasive Games (2007). Procedurality is the computer's principal value (p. 4); games persuade through the processes they model, which he calls procedural rhetoric. Use: procedurality, one pole of the third dichotomy.

[van-dijck-2018] van Dijck, Poell and de Waal, The Platform Society (2018). From the publisher's description (cite only for these points): platforms have become an organizing structure of Western societies, not merely services, and who anchors public values in them is contested between market, state and civil society. Use: datafication and the procedures that social media users insert themselves into, which chapter 8 takes up.

[murray-1997] Murray, Hamlet on the Holodeck (1997). From the author's notes: the digital environment has four essential properties, procedural, participatory, spatial and encyclopedic (p. 67). Use: the computer as a simulation machine, and procedurality as its defining quality, as digital writers see it.
