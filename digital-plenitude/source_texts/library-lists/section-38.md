[edwards-2007] Edwards, "The History of Civilization" (2007). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): Sid Meier's Civilization (1991), based on an earlier board game, has the player build a civilization from a simple agricultural stage to the present. Use: the game as history in procedural patchwork.

[aarseth-2004] Aarseth, "Genre Trouble" (2004). From the work's own use: "The computer game is the art of simulation." Use: games as simulations rather than stories.

[baudrillard-1994] Baudrillard, Simulacra and Simulation (1994). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): simulacra are imitations that have no original, have lost their connection to it, or are their own originals. Use: games as simulacra rather than scientific simulations.

[murray-1997] Murray, Hamlet on the Holodeck (1997). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): the pleasure of the Holodeck is a sealed universe that need not correspond to the world outside. Use: the pleasures of game worlds.

[murray-2004] Murray, "From Game-Story to Cyberdrama" (2004). From the work's own use: replay is "one of the most pleasurable and characteristic structures of computer-based gaming," and "a replay story world allows the interactor to experience all the possibilities of a moment, without privileging any one of them as the single choice." Use: replay, which history does not allow.

[lasch-1978] Lasch, The Culture of Narcissism (1978). From the work's own use: "We are fast losing the sense of historical continuity, the sense of belonging to a succession of generations originating in the past and stretching into the future." Use: a critic who saw the historical sense waning in the 1970s; he read it as decline, so say so when citing him.

[klein-2016] Klein, "Elon Musk Believes We Are Probably Characters in Some Advanced Civilization's Video Game" (2016). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): Musk has suggested that our world may itself be a simulation. Use: the paradigm of simulation carried to its limit.
