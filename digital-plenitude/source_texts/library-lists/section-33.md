[krieger-2015] Krieger, "The Politics of John Lennon's 'Imagine'" (2015). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): a videographer, John Callahan (Cal TV), set George W. Bush's speeches to the Wax Audio remix of Lennon's "Imagine." Use: political remix that turns a president's words against him.

[conti-2015] Conti, "Political Remix Videos as a Vernacular Discourse" (2015). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): political remix videos form their own ramifying series of genres. Use: the range of political remix.

[peverini-2015] Peverini, "Remix Practices and Activism" (2015). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): Greenpeace's 2008 mashup attacking Dove's owner over palm oil and tropical deforestation has had about two million views on YouTube. Use: activist remix and its audience.

[sinnreich-2010] Sinnreich, Mashed Up (2010). From the work's own use: configurable culture violates the principles of the "modern framework," which held that art is better than craft, an original of a better order than a copy, and artist and audience distinct (p. 68). Use: remix ignores the modern distinctions rather than critiquing them.

[lessig-2008] Lessig, Remix (2008). Girl Talk's Night Ripper is built from some 200 to 250 samples (pp. 11–15), and Gillis is a cardinal example of remix creativity (pp. 12–13). Use: Girl Talk as the exemplary remixer.

[watercutter-2008] Watercutter, "Mashup DJ Girl Talk Deconstructs Samples from Feed the Animals" (2008). From the work's own use (cite only for this point): Girl Talk's next album, Feed the Animals, had over three hundred samples. Use: the scale of Girl Talk's sampling.

[burger-1984] Bürger, Theory of the Avant-Garde (1984). The avant-garde aimed to reintegrate art into the practice of life (pp. 49–54); the neo-avant-garde institutionalizes it as art and so negates its intentions (p. 58). Use: the goal that eluded the historical avant-garde and that remixers such as Girl Talk achieve without seeking it.

[navas-2012] Navas, Remix Theory (2012). From the work's own use: "principles of Remix in new media blur the line between high and low culture … allowing average people and the elite to produce work with the very same tools." Use: remix and the end of the hierarchy of the arts.

[jameson-1991] Jameson, Postmodernism (1991). The postmodernisms efface the high-modernist frontier between high and mass culture (pp. 2–3), and pastiche takes the place of parody (pp. 16–17). Use: remix as popular postmodernism, which borrows like postmodern art without its dialogue with modernism.
