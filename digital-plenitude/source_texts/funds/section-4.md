[lessig-2008] Lessig, Remix (2008). Sousa feared that recorded music would turn a culture of amateur performance into one of consumption (pp. 23–27). The twentieth century was the first in which popular culture was professionalized, a "Read/Only" culture (pp. 28–29); digital technologies make "Read/Write" culture possible again (pp. 33–35). Girl Talk's Night Ripper is built from some 200 to 250 samples (pp. 11–15). Use: the technologies of recorded and streamed music, and the remix and sampling that cross the genres.

[levine-1988] Levine, Highbrow/Lowbrow (1988). From the publisher's description (cite only for these points): concert halls and opera houses were organised in the late nineteenth century to serve separate, elite publics, where earlier Shakespeare and opera had drawn every class. The book itself calls him "Laurence Levine"; the author is Lawrence W. Levine. Use: the founding of the great American orchestras.

[macdonald-1962] Macdonald, "Masscult and Midcult" (1960; page numbers from its 1962 printing in Against the American Grain). The postwar boom in orchestras and concert-going was real but passive, consuming rather than creating (pp. 58–61). His remedy: the mass audience is divisible into many smaller, specialized audiences, and "the more it is divided, the better" (pp. 72–74), though he wanted this to protect High Culture. Use: an opponent who foresaw the many musical communities and welcomed them for the opposite reason; say so when citing him.

[burger-1984] Bürger, Theory of the Avant-Garde (1984). After the historical avant-garde, the radically disparate exists side by side, and no movement can claim to be historically more advanced than any other (p. 63). Use: a theoretical statement of the condition in which 250 genres of rock coexist without a ranking.

[bolter-2022] Bolter, "The Condition of Reading in a Digital Media Culture" (2022). Bolter's own later chapter, carrying this argument on. Heterogeneity, not convergence on one medium, defines contemporary media culture (p. 36). Use: the later statement that musical tastes have never converged and digital media have multiplied the forms instead.

[chun-2021] Chun, Discriminating Data (2021). From the publisher's description (cite only for this point): recommender systems group people with others like them, so that the clusters of the digital world are partly made by algorithms built on "homophily." Use: the music bubble of streaming services, made in part by recommendation; a challenge to the picture of freely chosen communities.

[williams-1974] Williams, Television: Technology and Cultural Form (1974). From the publisher's description (cite only for these points): technology shapes television's cultural form without determining it, and Williams describes broadcasting as a planned "flow" of programmes. Use: the era of three networks and local radio chains, when musical tastes came closest to converging.

[manovich-2018] Manovich, AI Aesthetics (2018). Industrial-scale "cultural AI" now shapes the aesthetic choices of billions of people, and it models each user's "aesthetic self" from aggregated data to predict what that user will like (opening pages). Use: a development after 2019 that sharpens the question of musical bubbles: taste predicted and served by software.
